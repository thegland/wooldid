Design
Preregistered

Every configuration below generates data under a true null — no treatment effect at all, so the true event-study profile is flat and trivially lies on a line. A correctly sized test should then reject about 5% of the time. Rejecting far more often means the test manufactures pre-trends that aren't there; rejecting far less means it cannot see the ones that are.

Two criteria decide each case, and both must hold. The rejection rate must land inside a preregistered band, and the p-value distribution must pass a uniformity test. The second criterion is not ceremonial: a configuration from the earlier campaign sat at a comfortable-looking 2.4% while its p-value distribution was destroyed, because a structurally blind covariance matrix was dragged through a plausible rate by a lenient reference distribution. A rate inside the band is not, by itself, evidence of correctness.

The governing parameter

Clusters per cell — not cluster count, not cell size

The estimator fits a dummy for every treated cohort-by-period cell. When such a cell sits entirely inside one cluster, ordinary least squares forces that cell's residuals to sum to zero within the cluster, so the cluster-robust variance estimator becomes structurally blind to exactly the variation the line test depends on. Clustering finer than the cell breaks that blindness — but the campaign shows how much finer matters, and the answer is not obvious.

Two clusters per cell is not enough. With 60 clusters and 6-unit cells but only two clusters spanning each cell, rejection is 14.50% — far worse than the same 60 clusters arranged as 2-unit cells (9.10%), and worse than every configuration with six clusters per cell.

This was the campaign's decisive disagreement. One theorist predicted 14.5% against the others' 7.5–10.5%, named it as the result that would falsify his own account if it came back otherwise, and it landed at 14.50%. Any runtime guard should therefore be written on clusters-per-cell.

Three boundaries, stated plainly. The test needs cells that sit inside at least six clusters; it needs enough clusters overall (twenty is far too few); and it fails under cohort-by-period common shocks — notably even when such a shock contributes under 4% of error variance. That last one deserves emphasis in any documentation, because escaping the containment problem by clustering finer assumes away precisely the dependence that motivates cohort-level clustering in the first place, and nothing at runtime can detect it.

Power is not the constraint. Where size holds, planted deviations from a line were detected 98.8% and 100% of the time. The test's problem was never sensitivity.

Cell size

What larger cells do — and don't do

Enlarging cohort-by-period cells is the intuitive remedy: more observations behind every estimate ought to mean better inference. It does exactly that in one place, does nothing in another, and makes matters worse in three. The six configurations below hold cell size fixed at fifty observations and vary only the variance estimator and the error structure.

Two clusters per cell fails worse at fifty observations per cell than at six — 17.86% against 14.50%.

This is the clean separation the earlier result could not provide, because there cell size and clusters-per-cell moved together. Holding cells at fifty and varying only how many clusters span them confirms that clusters per cell is the governing quantity and cell size is not protective. A guard written on cell size would pass this configuration and be wrong.

Under cohort-period common shocks, larger cells are actively dangerous: 44.84% at six observations per cell becomes 98.02% at fifty.

The reason is that the shock is absorbed into each cell's own estimate and leaves no trace in the residuals, so no variance estimator can see it. The idiosyncratic part of the error shrinks in proportion to cell size while the shock does not, so the gap between the true and the estimated variance widens as cells grow. The predicted direction was right; the magnitude was not — this was the campaign's one badly missed prediction, locked at 65% and measured at 98%. The practical consequence is sharp: “use large cells and cluster finely” is sound advice only when there are no common shocks at the cohort-period level, and is close to worst-case advice when there are.

 Altmethod is better in every regime where the standard test fails, without exception, and the margin grows with the severity of the failure.

The reason is mechanical and worth recording: the standard statistic tests six dimensions after projecting out level and slope, while altmethod tests four. Fewer dimensions inverted through a directionally starved covariance matrix means less amplification — which predicts exactly the observed pattern, namely near-identical answers where the variance estimate is honest and uniformly smaller damage where it is not.

The one place altmethod changes a verdict is also the one place to be careful about it.

At 2-unit cells it certifies with 35 rejections out of 504 — and the band's upper limit is exactly 35. One more rejection would have failed it. On the identical paired datasets the comparison is 47 versus 35 (discordant 31 against 19), an improvement of 2.38 percentage points against a paired standard error of 1.40 — roughly 1.7 standard errors, suggestive but not decisive. Treat “small cells are supported under altmethod” as provisional until confirmed at a higher replication count. Everything else in the altmethod column reduces the magnitude of a failure without changing what it is.

Continuous treatments

The cell-size ladder

For continuous treatments the estimator fits a dose slope inside every treated cell, so each slope rests on however many observations that cell holds. Small cells mean shrunken residuals, understated variances, and a test that rejects far too often. Enlarging cells is therefore not a workaround — it is the mechanism running in reverse.

6 per cell31.55%
20 per cell9.92%
50 per cell5.95%

Excess over nominal falls from 26.6 to 4.9 to 0.95 percentage points — roughly a factor of five for each tripling of cell size. At fifty observations per cell the test is correctly sized (5.95%, uniformity 8.2), so the continuous version is conditional rather than irredeemable. The formal preregistered ruling, which keyed on the twenty-unit configuration, returns withdraw; the fifty-unit result arrived as a disclosed follow-up and by its own terms cannot overturn a trigger. The honest reading is a gated keep: permit above a cell-size threshold, refuse or caution below it, and route refused cases to randomization inference, bootstrap, or IM p-values. The threshold itself lies somewhere between twenty and fifty and has not been located.

The clustering advice inverts between the two sides.

Clustering finer than the cell is what rescues the binary test. For the continuous test it makes things worse: 48.41% under unit-level clustering against 31.55% under robust on identical geometry. Unit clustering manufactures noisy, few-degree-of-freedom off-diagonal terms in the covariance where robust structurally has zeros that happen to match the truth. One theorist predicted this against the other two.

Recommendations

What should ship

  • Guard on clusters per cell — never on cell size. Refuse outright when a treated cell lies inside a single cluster; caution below six clusters per cell, below roughly 180 clusters overall, or below six observations per cell under robust standard errors. Two clusters per cell fails at every cell size tested, so a size-based guard would wave it through.
  • Cover the covariance handoff, not just the test. The object at risk is the event-study covariance matrix that honestdid consumes; guarding the test alone relocates the failure rather than fixing it.
  • Gate the continuous version on cell size rather than removing it, and locate the threshold between twenty and fifty first.
  • Document the inverted advice — cluster finer for the binary test, do not for the continuous one.
  • Document the common-shock caveat prominently. It is undetectable at runtime, it defeats the finer-clustering escape route, and larger cells make it dramatically worse rather than better (98% rejection of a true null at fifty observations per cell). Any guidance to enlarge cells must carry this condition alongside it.
  • Never report undersizing as a pass. Robust standard errors under serial correlation rejected nothing at all across 504 replications.

Left open. Nothing certified below 180 clusters while 60 clusters came in marginal, so that boundary sits untested in between; the same is true of clusters-per-cell between two and six, and of the continuous threshold between twenty and fifty. Each needs one cheap follow-up before any number is documented as exact.

Provenance. Grid, per-configuration predictions and the keep-versus-abandon rule were committed before any replication ran; two follow-up additions are disclosed as such in the preregistration. Locked prediction bands contained the measured rate in 27 of 28 scored quantities.
