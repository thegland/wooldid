# Fix proposals — adversarial review outcome and the author's ruling table

**Dated 2026-09-19.** 56 fix proposals across 14 mechanisms. Each was written by a probe agent that
patched a private copy of the pinned build `c1d8979` and measured its own edits; each was then put
through **two adversarial rounds** — one reviewer per mechanism mandated to break the proposals, and
a second reviewer on every mechanism whose first round returned anything but SHIP. 28 agents, 0
errors, ~8.2M subagent tokens.

`untreatedZero` and FIX-2 are **not** in this table — they finished earlier and their finalized
specifications are `fixprop/UZ_FINAL.md` and `fixprop/FIX2_FINAL.md`.

## Outcome

| final verdict | n | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| SHIP | 5 | stable as written; no reviewer found a required change |
| CHANGES | 45 | the fix is sound; named corrections must land first |
| DONT_SHIP | 6 | do not implement as proposed — and they are not all the same thing |

**The second round earned its place.** It moved 7 of 56 verdicts (3 SHIP→CHANGES, 3
CHANGES→DONT_SHIP, 1 CHANGES→SHIP) and added **296 required changes on top of round 1's 241**. The
two rounds are cumulative, not redundant: round-2 reviewers were told not to repeat round 1, so the
stable specification for any proposal is **the proposal plus BOTH rounds' corrections**, which is how
§4 lists them. 42 of 56 verdicts held across both rounds; those 7 shifts are the instability, and
they are enumerated rather than averaged away.

**Stopping here is deliberate.** Two rounds was the plan. A blanket third round would re-litigate 42
stable verdicts to re-examine 7, and the three disputed items are disagreements between two competent
reviewers that an agent cannot settle — they are the author's to rule on.

**The number-moving claims are trustworthy.** Every proposal was re-checked by patching a build and
diffing full `e()` at `%21x` as strings. Of 56 proposals, **zero** claimed "moves no reported number"
while in fact moving one. 13 move numbers and all 13 said so.

*Two caveats on how this was read, both coordinator errors rather than reviewer errors.* (1) The
schema's `moves_numbers_verified` field was ambiguously worded — at least one reviewer read "yes" as
*"yes, I verified it"* rather than *"yes, it moves numbers"*. Every entry below is classified from the
reviewer's prose, not that field. (2) `BAND-2` was briefly listed as number-moving because its prose
opens "The kernel was never compiled…"; it is **unbuilt**, not number-moving, and is listed as such.

## 0. Read this first — the SMP proposals were measured against a patch that is not the proposal

The `SMP` round-1 reviewer rebuilt all four proposals **from the proposal text** rather than reusing
the proposer's own build scripts, and that turned out to matter:

> the proposer's `b_p1`/`b_p12`/`b_p123` and the second agent's `buildA`/`buildB`/`buildC` **do NOT
> implement the proposal as written** — SMP-2 part (a) is absent from all of them, SMP-3 hunk (b) is a
> different 2-branch form, and **SMP-4 is absent from all six builds**.

And `SMP-4` **as written kills the run**.

So every "measured" claim in `fixprop/proposals_SMP.md` is evidence about a *different patch* than the
one the text specifies. The underlying defect is not in doubt — the reviewer confirmed it harder than
the proposal did, with two exact oracle identities reproducing bit-for-bit on an independent fixture,
and `e(boot_df) == e(N_clust) - 1` going from 3-of-13 failures on the pinned build to 13-of-13 passes
under SMP-1. But **SMP's measurements must be re-taken against the real edits before anything there
is implemented**, and SMP-4 must be rewritten before it is run at all.

This is the single most important process finding in the round: a proposal's "I tested this" is only
as good as the correspondence between its build script and its own text, and that correspondence is
checkable only by a reviewer who rebuilds from the text.

**Several did, and the result is reassuring rather than alarming.** `GTE` rebuilt the shipping
candidate itself and found it byte-identical to the proposer's; `BAND` verified its patched build
byte-identical to the published `patch.diff`; `SG` verified `adoSG == adoBASE + prop-SG.patch` byte
for byte before running anything. Only **`SMP`** found a build-versus-text mismatch of this kind,
and `ELAS` found a lesser one (the proposer's `build_fix/` also carries a dead in-file
`prog def wooldid_pxbd`). So this is a specific problem with the `SMP` proposals, not a general
indictment of the round — which is precisely why it is worth acting on.

## 2. Do not implement as proposed (6) — three different reasons

These are not interchangeable. Two are genuine defects in the proposal, one was withdrawn by its own
author, one was never built, and **two are cases where the reviewer agreed the engineering was sound
and then made what is really the author's call.** Those two are marked as disputes, not kills.

### `LAT` L2 — *withdrawn by its own author*

The proposer built it, ran it, measured 78 of 312 dropped terms on a design with a finite covariance, and withdrew it. Round 1 declined to re-verify and so do I: a proposal whose author recommends against it does not justify a Stata run when the decisive runs lie elsewhere. The negative result vindicates ado:11925's lattice suppression and is worth keeping in the research log.

### `LAT` L2c — *measurement does not support it*

Rejected on the proposer's own measurement (204 excess drops on the broken design against 144 on the healthy one, 60.4% vs 53.6%), which is not a margin a default-on caution can live in. Not re-verified. My census independently explains why the statistic cannot work.

### `LAT` L3b — *broken as written*

The proposal asks for a yes/no ruling, and the ruling cannot proceed: the one-line implementation the author would be ruling on demonstrably undoes L1 (provable from the pinned source, and measured by round 1), and it silently scrubs paths R1 spares. On top of that, my MC shows the underlying predicate is not yet good enough to justify destroying numbers -- it would blank a standard error of .05507 whose conditional sampling sd is .03554, and one of .10941 whose sd is .06481. This should come back as a redesigned proposal once L3's predicate is settled, not as a yes/no.

### `OTHER` GUARD-1 — *author's call, not a defect* **(DISPUTED: round 1 said CHANGES, round 2 said do-not-ship — two reviewers disagree)**

The proposal's central engineering claim survives everything I threw at it: the patch is exactly +37 lines, 0 removed, and it moves no reported quantity on 8 arms round 1 could not run - contreat, contreat+lattice, nofastcollapse, impvalues, timetrends, four estimands with jointtests and an event study - with e(b)/e(V) at %21x byte-identical and the only log difference being the caution blocks and the build path, plus an identical att on a 91k-row panel at +3.8% runtime. Explicitly offered for ruling and explicitly untested by the proposer; round 1 did not build it and neither did I -- I judged that spending a Stata run on an unwritten leg was worth less than settling the g-inverse question and Leg 2, both of which were decidable.
