`dyex` is `dy/d ln x`: it logs the **regressor**, not the prediction. On a linear index
`xb`, `dyex = x * d(xb)/dx`, which is exactly linear in `b` and contains no prediction, so
the r(459)-on-a-negative-prediction hazard that motivates the `ey*` exclusion cannot arise
for it. The comment at 2062-2065 is wrong about `dyex` on its face; the question is whether
the numbers agree.

## 5. The identity, in the wooldid pipeline (the ruling's test)

`probe` build, contreat AME site (line 2547), ppmlhdfe fit, **k = 67 coefficients**, 300
subpopulation rows, aweighted. Identical numbers on p13, p23 and p24 (the AME read is the
same call in all three):

| quantity | `predict(xb)` (analytic) | `expression(predict(xb))` (retained) |
|---|---|---|
| `\|J*b - m\| / \|m\|` | **5.19e-16** | **2.67e-09** |
| `m` vs hand reference | **2.60e-16** | 4.81e-13 |
| max abs `J_form - J_analytic` | - (reference) | 2.81e-08 |
| ... relative to `max\|J_analytic\|` = 0.341 | - | 8.24e-08 |
| max relative, over non-zero `J` entries | - | 3.58e-08 |

Hand reference (non-circular): the weighted mean over the same 300 rows of
`x_i * [xb(x_i + 1) - xb(x_i)]`, built from two `predict, xb` passes bracketing an exact
+1 bump of the dose. The index is linear in the dose (`contreatpoly` 1), so the bump is an
exact slope with no step-size error. It agrees with the analytic margin to 2.6e-16, which
also confirms the probe reproduced margins' weighting and subpopulation exactly.

`m_analytic` vs `m_numerical` = 4.81e-13 - exactly the p13 movement in section 7 below, so
the whole end-to-end p13 difference is this one margin.

Standalone cross-checks (same do-file, `poisson` rather than `ppmlhdfe`):

| model | form | `m` vs hand | `J*b` vs `m` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `i.sg2##c.intensity i.trt##c.intensity i.t` (k=21) | `predict(xb)` | **1.16e-16** | **1.16e-16** |
| same | `expression(predict(xb))` | 6.05e-13 | 1.20e-08 |
| all-negative index (`xb < 0` on 1000/1000 rows), k=2 | `predict(xb)` | **0** (bit-exact) | **0** |
| same | `expression(predict(xb))` | 4.97e-13 | 8.40e-10 |

**The identity passes for `dyex` under the analytic form and fails under the retained one.**
Where the two disagree, the analytic form is the accurate one, judged against a reference
that is neither of them.

The negative-index probe also answers the comment's stated hazard directly: with the linear
index negative on every row, `dyex`, `eydx` and `eyex` all returned rc 0 under **both**
forms (8 calls, 8 zeros). No r(459) in either direction here. That does not refute the
"data-dependent, goes in both directions" claim for `ey*`; it does show the claim was never
about `dyex`, which never logs the prediction.

## 10. Conclusion

The identity **passes**. In the live pipeline the analytic form satisfies `J*b == margin` to
5.2e-16 and matches an independent hand construction to 2.6e-16; the retained numerical form
misses the identity by 2.7e-09 and carries a Jacobian error of ~3.6e-08 relative, which is
what the reported variances inherit. The stated rationale for holding `dyex` back - "both
code paths take ln of the prediction" - is false for `dyex` on its face and is not rescued
by an r(459) hazard (none fired in either form on an all-negative index).

With the gate extended, the cell collapse under poisson+dyex reproduces the full-data run on
the very fixture and configs the ban was written for: the **ame rows** - the rows the ban
exists to protect - move by <= 6.3e-16, against 1.2e-05 under the retained numerical form on
the identical run. The only residue left, 4.5e-12, sits on the `pretreatment:att` CI bound
and is the collapse's generic near-zero-bound round-off, already accepted on the analytic
paths. The ban's stated cause is the numerical form, and it goes away with it.

## 11. Recommendation for the author to rule on

1. **Extend the gate** (`wooldid.ado:2073`, the `poismarginstextC` line) to `dyex` - one
   added disjunct, exactly as the probe build has it:

   ```
   -    if "`contreatelasticitytype'" == "dydx" local poismarginstextC predict(xb)
   +    if "`contreatelasticitytype'" == "dydx" | "`contreatelasticitytype'" == "dyex" local poismarginstextC predict(xb)
   ```

   Line 2072 (`poismarginstext`, the main site) needs no change: `semielasticity` is only
   ever `dydx` or `eydx`, so that site never sees `dyex`. Cost: 1.9-28x faster here,
   5.9-31.2x on a quiet machine. Accuracy: strictly toward the exact value.
2. **Lift the poisson+dyex collapse ban** (`wooldid.ado:5074`) *only together with (1)* - the
   two must move in the same commit, since the ban is correct while the numerical form stands.
   The speed gain from (2) alone is small; the reason to do it is that a user-visible
   `error 198` under `fastcollapse` (and a silent skip otherwise) would otherwise persist for
   a cause that no longer exists.
3. **Text that must move with it** (all currently assert the retained numerical form):
   `wooldid.ado` 2062-2065 (the false rationale - at minimum restrict it to `ey*`),
   5065-5074 (the ban comment), 5118 (the poisson+dyex refusal bullet) and 5119
   ("dyex except under poisson - see above"); `wooldid.sthlp:338` and the eligibility
   sentence at `sthlp:346`; and the header of `test/test_poismargins.do` lines 5-9, which
   repeats the same false rationale verbatim.
4. **Certs that will fail and must be re-blessed**: `test/test_fastcollapse.do` F11c pins
   `poisson+dyex -> rc 198` (`BOPT5`/`BNM5` at :783-795, gate rc list at :839); the F11a
   family and the B-list counts around :776 change with it. `test/test_poismargins.do` P5
   exercises `eydx` only, so no cert pins the `dyex` **form** today - which is why the
   change is invisible to the suite unless a new pin is added. Recommend adding one:
   the `J*b == margin` identity on a poisson+contreat+dyex run, asserted at <= 1e-14.
5. **Do not touch the `ey*` branch.** Nothing here measures it, and its r(459) behaviour is
   deliberately preserved.
