## F. PS-62 resolved: the analytic covariance (2026-09-07, after the six-lens review)

**Review verdict.** All six lenses returned *feasible in part*, with the same split. The author's
skepticism was right about the general matrix and wrong about the core case.

- **Already computed, nothing to estimate.** Every reported relative-period effect of a family comes
  from ONE `margins` call (ado 2023, 2502, 2711, 3066), so that call's `r(V)` is their complete joint
  covariance, in both reference regimes and for the subgroup twins. wooldid captures it (2034-2035,
  2520-2521, 2731-2732, 3088-3089) and posts it as `e(V_es_*)` (3884). It never reaches `e(V)`, which is
  overwritten with diag(se^2) at 3794. Row alignment with the reported rows is already enforced by the
  shared positional filter in `wooldid_sglbl` (10478-10488), so a splice is mechanical.
- **Cheap but unread.** The cross-subgroup level covariance sits in one call's `r(V)`; margins also
  returns the full pairwise contrast covariance `r(V_vs)`, of which wooldid reads only `r(table_vs)`.
- **Blocked, do not build.** The general cross-family matrix. Three modes have no delta-method object at
  all: `impvalues` (per-cluster t-test SEs under `nose`), `unconditionalse` (a linearization variance, not
  J V J'), and `fastnose` (no variance anywhere). Rank deficiency is guaranteed, not hypothetical: the
  main effect is a linear combination of the horizons, pinned at 6e-17 by identity_extras E4.
- **Comparator calibration.** wooldid is not uniquely deficient. One comparator ships diag(se^2) on its
  bootstrap path, another gives its per-cohort rows no covariance, a third keeps its aggregated event
  study out of `e()` entirely.

**Rulings.**

| question | ruling |
|---|---|
| scope | **Splice in the event-study blocks only.** The within-family event-study covariance goes into the corresponding diagonal blocks of `e(V)`; every other off-diagonal stays zero. No new estimation, and a test across horizons becomes valid. |
| labelling | **Help text plus a stored note.** Document the mixed matrix in the help, and store the warning as an `e()` macro. Deliberately NOT a footer under the printed table. |
| sentinel | **Zero the sentinel row and column.** Where a row's variance is unidentified, zero its row and column rather than carrying the -1e100 sentinel into a matrix with off-diagonals, which the posting screen can zero wholesale. |

**Consequence for PS-45 (simultaneous bands).** The condition the author attached is now met for the
event-study case specifically: the covariance those bands need exists on the analytic path and the full
replications-by-horizon draw matrix exists on the bootstrap path. Awaiting his go/no-go.

**The add-one case, as put to the author.** For: under a permutation null the observed assignment is
exchangeable with the permuted ones, so it belongs in the reference set and counts itself; this is what
makes the p exactly valid at any draw count, where the plain share over-rejects (measured: six percent at
a nominal five with ninety-nine replications, on the studentized tail); it cannot print zero, its floor
honestly reporting the resolution; and it is the sampled-draw analogue of exhaustive enumeration, where
the observed assignment is a member by construction. Against: the plain share is the unbiased estimator
of the tail probability, is what `ritest` and `permute` report, and the difference is immaterial at large
draw counts. Decisive distinction: exactness holds for permutation and rank-based null tests, but the
bootstrap percentile distribution is centred at the estimate rather than the null, so there add-one is a
conservatism choice rather than an exactness result - which is why the split is defensible and only needed
documenting.

## I. P7 resolved: engine parity is the wrong goal (2026-09-08)

Six-lens research, report at `research_2026-09-08/engine_parity.md`. Across all lens-item votes:
**59 principled, 43 incidental, 4 unclear**; recommendations **73 keep-and-document against 27
harmonize**. The sweep's P7 framing, which treats parity as the goal, is not supported.

Three mechanical facts generate the legitimate differences, and every difference that does not trace to
one of them is incidental: **recentring** (the bootstrap must manufacture a null; permutation draws are
null draws and the observed statistic belongs to its own reference set), **reweighting** (a bootstrap
replication's standard error is a different functional of the data; relabelling onset leaves the
functional unchanged), and **the assignment being the randomized object** (anything keyed on treatment
status has no permutation distribution at all).

| question | ruling |
|---|---|
| disposition | **Document the differences, harmonize only the accidents.** The goal is justified difference, not parity: publish why the engines differ, grounded in the three facts, and change behaviour only where nothing statistical requires it. |
| sweep errata | **Corrected in place.** Four entries amended in `consistency_sweep_2026-09-06.md`: CS-113 and CS-48 rested on false premises, CS-96 was called indefensible but is correct once explained, and CS-32's verdict was backwards. |
| the accidents | **Take all four.** Give `permute()` its own exchangeability caution rather than porting the bootstrap's few-cluster ones, since the failure modes differ in kind; write the plot-data file on the analytic path too; give both compatibility lists one shape with reasons; complete the fast-mode help entry with the options that refuse it. |
| bootstrap step-down | **Keep it permutation-only.** The earlier ruling stands even though Romano and Wolf is itself a bootstrap procedure and the substrate exists: the simultaneous bands give the bootstrap its multiplicity device for the same family. |

**The two engines' exchangeability assumptions are orthogonal, not one strong and one weak.** The pairs
cluster bootstrap assumes clusters are exchangeable as SAMPLING draws and fails with few or atypical
treated clusters. Randomization inference assumes clusters are exchangeable in ASSIGNMENT and fails when
treated clusters are heterogeneous in variance. Same shape, different failure modes, different remedies -
which is why the bootstrap's existing referral to `permute()` is a hand-off, not a clean bill of health,
and why the permutation caution must be written from its own failure mode rather than copied.

**Why the adversarial ruling is safe to make now.** The shipped test is exactly level-valid for the null it
states - measured size .0508 against a pure reference-period shift over 40,000 draws - so this is not a
size failure. But the one-parameter design space is nested in the two-parameter one, so its noncentrality
is at least as large for EVERY alternative; that is a theorem of nested minimisation, not a simulation
result, and 20,000 random smooth alternatives produced no exception. The price is one degree of freedom,
bounded at 1.2 to 6.4 percentage points. The crossover is at s_r ~ s_h, and a realistic 600-cluster design
sits at 0.476, inside the region where the one-parameter form wins. The demonstration: a design carrying a
five-unit anomaly at each cohort's own reference period returns every event-study coefficient near -5 with
|t| between 40.8 and 67.9 and a joint pre-trend p of 0.0000, while the shipped adversarial test returns
p = .7477 analytic, .8293 under boottstat and .9016 under permute. Its power against a reference-period
anomaly of ANY size is exactly .05.
